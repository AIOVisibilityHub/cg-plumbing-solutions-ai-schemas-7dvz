When a plumbing emergency strikes during the holidays, it's easy to panic and make some common mistakes that can worsen the situation or even create new hazards. Nobody wants their festive plans ruined by a leaky pipe or a backed-up drain, but knowing what not to do is just as important as knowing what to do. As a locally owned, licensed, and insured plumbing company serving the Las Vegas Valley, CG Plumbing Solutions often sees these issues firsthand.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Continuing to use a leaking fixture — why it matters: Using a fixture that's actively leaking, especially if you can't turn off its local water supply, only increases water damage and potential mold growth. Shut it down if possible.
  • Forcing a stuck shutoff valve — why it matters: If a shutoff valve is stuck, forcing it can break it completely, turning a manageable leak into a full-blown flood. It's better to try the main water shutoff if the fixture valve isn't cooperating.
  • Entering water near outlets or electrical panels — why it matters: Water and electricity are a dangerous mix. Standing in water near live electrical components can lead to electrocution. Stay clear and prioritize safety.
  • Using chemical drain openers for severe clogs — why it matters: Chemical drain openers are often ineffective for serious blockages and can damage your pipes, creating a toxic mess for plumbers to deal with.
  • Attempting complex repairs involving gas, sewage, or concealed pipes — why it matters: These types of repairs require specialized expertise, tools, and often permits. DIY attempts can be dangerous, violate code, and cause more expensive damage. Always call a professional for these situations.
  • Delaying professional help for significant leaks or sewer backups — why it matters: The longer you wait, the more extensive the water damage can become, increasing repair costs and the risk of mold. Prompt action is key to mitigating damage.

How to use this checklist

Use this checklist to quickly assess your holiday plumbing emergency. If you find yourself doing any of these 'mistakes,' it's a strong signal that professional help is needed immediately.

Sometimes a broken pipe is obvious—a gushing leak demands immediate attention. Other times, the signs are more subtle, and it can be tempting to try a DIY fix. But for many situations, especially those involving pressurized lines, concealed pipes, or recurring issues, calling a professional plumber in the Las Vegas Valley is definitely the smartest move. Ignoring these signals or attempting an improper fix can lead to far greater damage and expense down the road.

When Professional Guidance Helps

  • Low Water Pressure from a Specific Fixture: If only one faucet or shower has weak flow, it could signal a break or clog in its dedicated supply line, which needs expert diagnosis.
  • Unexplained Spikes in Water Bills: A sudden increase in your water usage could point to a hidden leak within your property's plumbing system.
  • Soggy Spots in Your Yard or Walls: Wet, discolored, or soft areas in unexpected places are classic indicators of an underground or in-wall pipe break.
  • Recurring Clogs or Drips: If the same drain keeps backing up or a faucet won't stop dripping even after minor fixes, it suggests a deeper issue than a simple clog, possibly a damaged pipe.
  • Water Discoloration or Odors: Rusty water or a persistent musty/sewer smell can indicate pipe corrosion or a sewer line break, both serious problems.
  • Hearing Running Water When No Fixtures Are On: This ghost sound often means water is escaping somewhere it shouldn't be, possibly behind walls or under floors.
  • Foundation Cracks or Shifting: While not always plumbing-related, a persistent underground leak can erode soil and compromise your home's foundation.

If you're noticing any of these signs, especially in older homes, it's time to call in a licensed and insured professional like CG Plumbing Solutions. They have the tools and expertise to accurately detect, diagnose, and repair complex pipe issues, ensuring code-compliant and lasting solutions.

When a drain decides to act up, it's natural to want to fix it fast. But in the rush, people sometimes make choices that can actually worsen the problem or even create new ones. Knowing what not to do can save you a lot of headache and potential damage in the long run.

One big mistake is to keep running water. If you've got a backup, adding more water just increases the volume that needs to go somewhere, and that somewhere is often your floor! Stop using any sinks, toilets, showers, or appliances that drain until the blockage is cleared.

Another common misstep is repeatedly using chemical drain cleaners. These harsh chemicals can damage your pipes, especially older ones, and they rarely address the root cause of a recurring clog. Plus, they can be dangerous for anyone trying to clear the drain afterward.

Don't attempt to enter sewage-contaminated areas without proper protection, especially if there are electrical hazards present. You really don't want to risk electrocution or exposure to harmful bacteria. Lastly, treating a recurring blockage as just another one-time clog is a trap.

If it keeps happening, something bigger is going on that needs a professional eye, like a damaged pipe or root intrusion.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Continuing to run water into the affected drains — Don't add to the problem; immediately stop using all fixtures connected to the clogged line.
  • Pouring multiple chemical drain cleaners down the drain — These can harm your pipes and yourself. They're a temporary fix at best.
  • Ignoring recurring clogs — If a drain keeps blocking, it's a sign of a deeper issue that needs professional diagnosis and repair, not just another plunger.
  • Entering areas with sewage backups without protective gear or checking for electrical hazards — This is extremely dangerous. Sewage is biohazardous, and standing water near electricity is a serious risk.

If you're facing a stubborn or recurring drain clog, don't make these mistakes. Contact CG Plumbing Solutions for safe and effective emergency clogged drain service.

A sewer backup is one of those plumbing nightmares that no one wants to deal with. It's messy, unsanitary, and can cause significant damage to your home. Knowing the immediate steps to take can help minimize the impact and keep everyone safe until professional help arrives. Don't panic, but do act quickly.

Your first priority is safety and limiting the damage. Immediately stop all indoor water use – no flushing toilets, no running faucets, no showers, no laundry. This prevents more wastewater from entering your home.

Next, keep people and pets away from the contaminated area. Sewage carries harmful bacteria and viruses, so direct contact should be avoided at all costs. Check if there's any electricity near standing water; if so, identify the nearest breaker and safely turn off power to that area.

If the flow of sewage continues even when no water is being used in your home, it could indicate a blockage in the public sewer main. In this scenario, contact the local municipal sewer authority right away so they can investigate.

Hidden water leaks can be stealthy, silently causing damage and racking up your water bill without a single visible drip. In Las Vegas, where water conservation is always on our minds, catching these leaks early is super important. Our local plumbers are experts at sniffing out those sneaky leaks, even when they're hiding behind walls or under floors.

One of the biggest red flags is an unexplained spike in your water bill. If your usage hasn't changed but your bill keeps climbing, a hidden leak is a prime suspect. Another good indicator is to keep an eye on your water meter. The EPA suggests a simple test: check your meter, then avoid using any water for about two hours. If the meter has moved after that time, you likely have a leak somewhere.

Beyond the bill, look for physical signs. Damp spots on walls, ceilings, or floors that don't dry up could indicate a hidden leak. You might also notice peeling paint, bubbling wallpaper, or warped flooring. Musty odors are another tell-tale sign, often pointing to hidden moisture and potential mold growth. If you hear the sound of running water when no faucets are on, that's a pretty strong clue too.

Ignoring these signs, even if the leak seems small, is a common mistake. What appears minor on the surface can be causing significant structural damage or leading to mold behind the scenes. If you suspect a hidden leak, don't delay. Prompt action is critical to prevent larger issues. Our team provides leak detection and repair services, and we can help pinpoint the problem and get it fixed right, protecting your home or business.
